Media over QUIC

An IETF transport for live media built on QUIC's multiplexed, loss-tolerant streams and built-in TLS 1.3. Publish/subscribe tracks and relay fan-out deliver sub-second, glass-to-glass latency on the open internet.

🌐

moq.pro CDN

Vivoh.Earth streams ride moq.pro, Luke Curley's hosted Media-over-QUIC network. Global relays with WebTransport and automatic WebSocket fallback, plus built-in reconnect — so a broadcast plays in every modern browser, including iPhone, and rides out flaky networks.

How it works | Server Status

When you go live, your browser captures and encodes the media with native WebCodecs, encrypts every frame, and publishes it to the moq.pro CDN over QUIC. Viewers subscribe from the nearest relay and decrypt in the page. The CDN carries only ciphertext it cannot read.

The key never reaches us. It is derived in your browser from the part of the share link after the #, which browsers never send to a server. We could not watch your broadcast if we were asked to.

The other side of that: the link is the key, so anyone it reaches can decrypt. What stops them is Require sign-in, which is on by default — we refuse to connect anyone who is not signed in, and you see exactly who is watching. Be clear about the difference: the encryption does not depend on us and cannot be undone by us, while the access control does and can. We are in a position to grant it, and in principle to be compelled to.

Each broadcast is reached through a short-lived moq.pro access token minted by Vivoh.Earth's own Cloudflare Worker. It is signed with a key whose public half alone was given to moq.pro, so the CDN can check our tokens and cannot forge one. Live chat is encrypted the same way: messages and display names are sealed in your browser under a key derived from the same link, so the chat server relays text it cannot read. That also means there is no moderation of chat — there is nothing for us to moderate.
