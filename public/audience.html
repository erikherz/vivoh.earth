Each row is a viewing session — one browser tab, and how long it watched. It is not a person. Nothing here is stable across sessions, so two rows can never be shown to be the same human, on one stream or across streams. One viewer who reloads counts twice; that is the cost of not keeping an identifier that could be demanded from us later.
Unmeasured sessions. Sessions recorded before heartbeats existed have no length — we know they happened and nothing more. They are shown separately and left out of every average, because counting them as zero-second views would drag each stream's average toward nothing on the strength of rows that were never measured. No new session can be unmeasured; the count only ever falls.
